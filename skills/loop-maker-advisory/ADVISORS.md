# Advisory Framework Catalog

This catalog contains concise, educational summaries of analytical frameworks associated with the thinkers listed below. It is a routing aid, not a set of claims that these people endorse Loop Maker or would give the generated advice.

Use the lenses as questions and tests. Do not imitate a person's voice, invent quotations, attribute generated conclusions to them, or treat the profiles as substitutes for primary sources.

## Strategy

### `drucker` — Peter Drucker: customer value and effectiveness
**Use when:** deciding what to build, whom to serve, where to allocate effort, or what to stop.
- Who is the customer, and what value do they actually recognize or pay for?
- Are we doing the right thing, or merely doing an activity efficiently?
- What should be abandoned to release capacity for higher-value work?
- What concrete contribution makes this initiative worth doing?
**Brake:** projects without a clearly identified customer, beneficiary or meaningful contribution.

### `munger` — Charlie Munger: multidisciplinary judgement
**Use when:** a high-stakes decision may be distorted by narrow thinking, incentives or hidden downside.
- Invert the problem: what would guarantee failure?
- Who has which incentives, and how could those incentives distort behavior?
- What are the second- and third-order consequences?
- Is the decision inside our circle of competence?
- Does it retain a margin of safety under pessimistic assumptions?
- Which cognitive biases could be affecting the decision?
**Brake:** sunk-cost escalation, unexamined incentives and fragile plans that work only under optimistic assumptions.

### `buffett` — Warren Buffett: durable advantage and focus
**Use when:** evaluating strategic focus, investment of scarce resources or defensibility.
- What durable advantage exists: brand, switching costs, network effects, cost advantage or another defensible asset?
- Is the advantage demonstrable or merely asserted?
- What should receive concentrated attention, and what should be declined?
- Does the opportunity fit our competence and time horizon?
**Brake:** diversification, expansion or commitments before the core is defensible.

## Competition

### `helmer` — Hamilton Helmer: structural competitive power
**Use when:** testing whether an advantage can persist despite competitors.
- What specific source of power is claimed?
- Does it create a material benefit and a barrier that prevents competitors from arbitraging it away?
- Consider scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resources and process power.
- What evidence would show that the claimed power is real and durable?
**Brake:** vague claims of differentiation without a measurable benefit and barrier.

### `christensen` — Clayton Christensen: disruption and jobs-to-be-done
**Use when:** evaluating market entry, product innovation or threats from simpler alternatives.
- Is this sustaining innovation or potentially disruptive innovation?
- Could an overlooked low-end or new-market segment be served by a simpler offer?
- What job is the customer trying to get done in a specific circumstance?
- Which incumbent incentives make it rational to ignore an emerging threat?
**Brake:** competing head-on with an incumbent without understanding the customer's job or the market's disruption path.

### `sunzi` — Sun Tzu: strategic positioning and choice of battles
**Use when:** facing an adversary, competitor, negotiation or constrained strategic environment.
- Should this contest be fought at all?
- What do we know—and not know—about our capabilities, the other side and the environment?
- Where is the terrain favorable, and where can indirect positioning avoid a costly confrontation?
- What action changes the conditions rather than merely reacting?
**Brake:** frontal contests, reactive escalation and action without adequate information.

## Product and experience

### `jobs` — Steve Jobs: product experience and subtraction
**Use when:** deciding product scope, quality, user experience or what to remove.
- What is the end-to-end experience for a first-time user?
- What can be removed without damaging the core value?
- Is the product coherent as an experience rather than a pile of features?
- Which quality gap would make the result unacceptable to its intended audience?
**Brake:** feature bloat and shipping complexity without a clear user benefit.

### `hara` — Kenya Hara: essence, emptiness and clarity
**Use when:** redesigning familiar systems, communication, interfaces or experiences.
- What remains if accumulated assumptions and decoration are stripped away?
- Can the familiar be examined with fresh eyes?
- Does the design leave room for the user, or impose unnecessary meaning and complexity?
- What is the simplest expression of the underlying purpose?
**Brake:** complexity and decoration that obscure the essence.

### `eyal` — Nir Eyal: behavior and habit design
**Use when:** designing repeated user behavior, onboarding, retention or engagement.
- What internal or external trigger starts the behavior?
- Is the action simple enough to perform?
- What variable reward is genuinely valuable rather than manipulative?
- What investment makes future use more useful?
- Is the habit-forming mechanism ethical and aligned with user interests?
**Brake:** engagement loops that exploit users without delivering meaningful value.

### `kahneman` — Daniel Kahneman: cognitive bias and decision design
**Use when:** people must choose, interpret risk, compare options or act under uncertainty.
- What does intuitive, fast judgement notice first, and what requires deliberate reasoning?
- Are framing, anchoring, availability, loss aversion or base-rate neglect distorting judgement?
- Are probabilities and reference classes being considered appropriately?
- Does the interface or process account for actual human cognitive limits?
**Brake:** decisions that assume people are perfectly rational or that ignore predictable biases.

### `karpathy` — Andrej Karpathy: empirical AI capability judgement
**Use when:** evaluating AI feasibility, model-based workflows, autonomy or AI architecture.
- What exact task must the model perform, and how has that task been evaluated?
- Where are capabilities jagged or brittle?
- What happens on edge cases, distribution shifts and repeated runs?
- Which parts should be automated, assisted or kept under human verification?
- What are the latency, cost, reliability and failure-recovery implications?
**Brake:** AI adoption based on demos, hype or broad capability claims without task-specific evaluation.

## Growth and distribution

### `chen` — Andrew Chen: cold start and growth mechanics
**Use when:** launching a networked product, marketplace, platform or growth loop.
- What is the smallest atomic network that delivers value?
- Which side of the market is hardest to acquire?
- What type of network effect or growth loop actually exists?
- What must be true for acquisition and retention to reinforce one another?
**Brake:** scaling acquisition before the product's core network or retention loop works.

### `godin` — Seth Godin: positioning and smallest viable audience
**Use when:** choosing an audience, positioning an offer or earning attention.
- Who is this specifically for—and who is it not for?
- Is the offer remarkable to that audience?
- What shared belief, identity or problem makes the audience care?
- Is attention earned through permission and value rather than interruption?
**Brake:** generic positioning aimed at everyone and growth based only on louder promotion.

### `miller` — Donald Miller: customer-centered narrative
**Use when:** clarifying a value proposition, website, pitch or marketing message.
- Who is the customer as the protagonist?
- What external, internal and philosophical problem do they face?
- How does the organization act as a credible guide?
- Is there a clear plan and call to action?
- Can the message communicate the value quickly and plainly?
**Brake:** messaging that makes the organization the hero or leaves the customer confused.

### `lois` — George Lois: creative concept and memorability
**Use when:** evaluating a campaign, concept, communication or creative direction.
- Is there one central idea strong enough to carry the work?
- Does it provoke attention for a relevant reason, not merely shock?
- Can it be expressed simply and remembered?
- Is the creative choice distinctive while remaining truthful and appropriate?
**Brake:** safe, generic creative work that communicates nothing distinctive.

## Execution and startup

### `musk` — Elon Musk: first-principles decomposition and execution
**Use when:** confronting assumed constraints, technical feasibility or slow execution.
- Which constraints are fundamental, and which are inherited conventions?
- Can the problem be decomposed into basic components, costs and dependencies?
- What can be simplified or eliminated before optimizing?
- What is the fastest safe experiment that tests the critical assumption?
- Where would speed increase risk beyond acceptable limits?
**Brake:** accepting inherited constraints without analysis—or using speed as an excuse to skip validation and safety.

### `pg` — Paul Graham: early-stage learning and product-market fit
**Use when:** starting a product, validating an idea or finding initial users.
- Are we making something a specific group of people genuinely wants?
- What can be learned by speaking directly with users?
- Which unscalable manual action would accelerate learning now?
- Are we optimizing or scaling before the core value is proven?
- Is there a founder or team insight that provides unusual access to the problem?
**Brake:** building at scale before evidence of genuine user demand.

## Routing hints

| Decision pattern | Strong starting perspectives |
|---|---|
| Should we do this? Is it worth it? | drucker, munger, buffett |
| Competitive strategy or price pressure | helmer, christensen, sunzi, buffett |
| Product scope or experience | jobs, hara, kahneman |
| Habit, onboarding or retention | eyal, kahneman, jobs |
| AI feasibility or architecture | karpathy, munger, jobs |
| Growth, marketplace or network effects | chen, godin, helmer |
| Positioning, messaging or campaign | godin, miller, lois |
| Early-stage product or startup | pg, drucker, chen |
| Execution under constraints | musk, munger, drucker |

These are starting points, not mandatory combinations. Select only perspectives that add distinct decision-relevant insight.
